How I work

Budget Management

Spending other people's money as if it were my own, and defending it with numbers.

A budget is a set of bets

Every line in a budget is a bet that this spend returns more than the alternative. I frame budget decisions as trade-offs, not entitlements, so the conversation is about impact per euro, not last year's baseline.

Forecast, then track the variance

The plan is a hypothesis and the variance is the learning. I track actuals against forecast monthly and care most about the gap. A 20 percent overrun I predicted is fine, a 5 percent surprise is a process problem.

Vendors and contracts are levers

A big chunk of program cost is external: tooling, agencies, audits. I negotiate scope and terms actively and revisit renewals rather than auto paying, because unmanaged vendor spend is where budgets quietly leak.

Tie spend to outcomes, not activity

I resist funding activity, such as we need three more contractors, and fund outcomes, such as we need the audit done by Q3. It keeps the budget honest and makes cuts, when they come, a scope conversation instead of a headcount fight.

Real example

Running agency and delivery work at Hakuhodo i-Studio, I owned project budgets against fixed client envelopes. Protecting margin meant tight scope control and early escalation on change requests, the discipline that keeps a fixed price project from becoming a loss.
