How I work

Web3 Product Management

Managing products at the intersection of protocol, finance, and user experience.

What makes Web3 PM different

Web3 product management combines traditional PM skills with deep domain knowledge in blockchain architecture, token economics, decentralized governance, and smart contract constraints. The biggest shift: in Web3, your 'users' include end users, developers building on your protocol, validators, liquidity providers, and token holders - each with different needs and incentive structures. A decision that improves UX for end users might create friction for developers. Navigating these trade-offs requires understanding the full ecosystem.

My Web3 background

At Datachain I served as Senior Project Manager and Product Owner for the deployment of the first IBC (Inter-Blockchain Communication) bridge connecting Ethereum and BNB Chain ecosystems. IBC is the Cosmos protocol for cross-chain communication - adapting it for EVM-compatible chains required working with core protocol developers, smart contract engineers, security auditors, and institutional banking partners. I defined the product vision, managed the roadmap, wrote 90+ user stories, and coordinated 3 cross-functional teams across 2 timezones for 12 months.

Key concepts I work with

Cross-chain bridges and IBC protocol. Smart contract lifecycle (development → audit → testnet → mainnet). DeFi primitives: liquidity pools, AMMs, lending protocols, yield mechanisms. EVM vs non-EVM architectures. Wallet UX and transaction signing flows. Gas optimization as a product constraint. On-chain analytics and chain explorer data. Tokenomics basics: utility tokens, governance tokens, staking mechanics.

Security as a product requirement

In Web3, security is not a QA phase - it is a product requirement from day one. Every feature that interacts with smart contracts must have a threat model. I work with security auditors as design partners, not final reviewers. At Datachain, I built audit timelines into every roadmap as hard gates (not estimates), maintained a pre-deployment security checklist with 47 items, and ran a mandatory 2-week testnet stability period before every mainnet release. Result: 0 critical incidents on the IBC bridge mainnet launch.

From Web3 back to traditional product

My Web3 experience adds specific value in traditional product contexts: extreme precision in specification (smart contracts have no 'we'll fix it in the next sprint' safety net), comfort with technical complexity, stakeholder management across highly heterogeneous groups, and a data-first mindset built on on-chain transparency. These practices transfer directly to regulated industries (fintech, medtech) and platform products where reliability and trust are product features.
